# How should the app respond when a student asks for the full solution?

**Decision:** Whether solutions are given on request, after an attempt, or step by step, and how the app makes it visible that a shortcut skipped the learning. Shows up in every tutoring session.

**Why it's uncertain:** Principle 6 keeps solutions unlocked, but evidence suggests unrestricted answer access raises practice scores while lowering later exam performance (lead: Bastani et al. 2025 in PNAS, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning", which has a published correction and a critique). Principle 1 needs the student to do the thinking.

## Sub-questions

- Does access to full solutions during practice harm delayed exam performance, and under which conditions?
- Which guardrails (attempt first, graded hints, self-explanation prompts) preserve learning without locking solutions?
- Does making the cost of shortcuts visible change student behavior?

## What would change the design

If any answer access reliably harms learning, principle 6 needs friction by default. If guardrails like attempt-first prompts suffice, solutions can stay one tap away.

Not researched yet.
