# How much should the app steer when a student's own choices work against their learning?

**Decision:** How strongly recommendations push back when a student skips reviews, blocks practice on one topic, or picks rereading over retrieval, and which choices the plan simply follows.

**Why it's uncertain:** Principle 5 gives the student control, but students reliably prefer massed practice and rereading and judge them as more effective than they are.

## Sub-questions

- When does learner control in instructional software help or hurt outcomes?
- How accurate are students' judgments of which study strategies work for them?
- Which steering techniques (defaults, explanations, showing evidence of their own forgetting) change choices without reducing autonomy and motivation?

## What would change the design

If learner control mostly hurts, defaults and nudges must be stronger than principle 5 suggests. If informed choice works, explaining recommendations is enough.
