# Q-013: What units should the topic map track, and how should the pipeline build it from the parsed material?

## Answer

The map needs two layers.

- **Knowledge components (KCs)** are the unit that items, hints, source chunks and attempts are tagged to. A KC is defined by the tasks that show it, at the level just above what students in the course can already do ([Koedinger 2012](../sources/koedinger-2012-kli-framework.md)).
- **Topics** group KCs. They are the unit the planner prioritises and the readiness view reports on. One student produces far too little data for reliable per-KC estimates before an exam ([Pelánek 2017](../sources/pelanek-2017-learner-modeling-overview.md)).

The fine layer still earns its place: a sub-skill the map fails to name never gets targeted practice, and fixing one such gap improved learning in a classroom experiment ([Liu 2017](../sources/liu-2017-closing-the-loop.md)).

LLMs can draft KCs and prerequisite links at "useful first draft" quality ([Moore 2024](../sources/moore-2024-llm-kc-generation.md), [Fan 2025](../sources/fan-2025-llm-kc-learning-curves.md), [Yang 2024](../sources/yang-2024-llm-concept-graph-recovery.md)). Their grain errors run in either direction depending on model and domain, and their prerequisite judgments are wrong often enough that edges must stay soft. No study has built a map end to end from one course's material, so map quality has to be measured in the app.

Knowledge kinds (facts, rules, principles) are a plausible but largely untested guide to item design; they should not drive scheduling logic.

Data-driven repair of the map works only when many students share it.

Overall confidence: **C** for the two-layer unit and the build pipeline, **D** for the knowledge-kind and repair parts in our setting.

## Sub-questions

### What unit should mastery be tracked on, and at what granularity do estimates stay reliable?

**The tagging unit is the knowledge component.** A KC is "an acquired unit of cognitive function or structure that can be inferred from performance on a set of related tasks"; concept, principle, fact and skill are all KCs ([Koedinger 2012](../sources/koedinger-2012-kli-framework.md), p. 9).

Two consequences for the map:

- A node that no practice task can show is not a KC, so the map has to be anchored on tasks and not only on the headings of the material.
- The right grain is the level at which students in the course still make errors, "a single level that lies just above the level at which novices have achieved success and fluency" (p. 10).

**Why the fine layer matters.** In Liu & Koedinger's classroom experiment, one expert KC ("circle area") hid a harder sub-skill: finding the radius from the area needs a square root. Splitting it into its own knowledge-traced KC, with a matching hint and scaffold, raised immediate post-test scores from 0.73 to 0.85 (d = 0.47) ([Liu 2017](../sources/liu-2017-closing-the-loop.md), pp. 33–34). The gain came entirely from the three targeted items.

What this shows is that an _unaddressed sub-skill_ costs learning. The KC split was confounded with the new hint, and the test came one day after practice. It is evidence for naming sub-skills in the map, so the item bank and the tutor can target them. It is not evidence that per-KC mastery estimates help.

**Why estimates belong at topic level.** Finer KCs mean "fewer data per KC, and thus worse consistency of parameter estimates" ([Pelánek 2017](../sources/pelanek-2017-learner-modeling-overview.md), p. 319). Every grain-size result here comes from systems with many students per KC; Liu's validation used 82 students and 72,404 steps (p. 28).

Our launch setting is one student per course with days to weeks of practice. A per-KC estimate will mostly reflect its prior. The review's standard compromise is a hierarchy of KCs under coarser topics (p. 331), and that fits our case:

- **Topic level:** the planner and readiness view use estimates here, shown with their uncertainty.
- **KC level:** coverage ("practised / not yet practised") and item selection, with KC estimates that borrow strength from their topic.

**How large the map can be.** Across 27 datasets of tutor practice, students start at about 65% correct after lectures and gain about 2.5 percentage points per practice opportunity, with feedback after each step ([Koedinger 2023](../sources/koedinger-2023-learning-rate-regularity.md), abstract only).

The qualitative consequence is that each KC needs repeated practice, so the number of KCs a student can work through is bounded by the time before the exam. A map with hundreds of nodes for a ten-day plan cannot be practised node by node. We do not turn this into a number: the figures are population averages, come from step-level tutoring with immediate feedback, and say nothing about forgetting before a delayed exam.

Grade: **C** for KCs as the tagging unit with topics above them (theory, a review, one classroom RCT, medium transfer). **D** for any specific granularity or map-size number.

### Which knowledge types need distinct treatment, and does each need different practice and scheduling?

**Three kinds.** The KLI framework distinguishes three kinds of KC ([Koedinger 2012](../sources/koedinger-2012-kli-framework.md), Table 3, p. 12):

- **Facts and definitions:** constant condition and response. In post-school math "relatively minor components … but they can be significant barriers" (p. 13).
- **Rules, procedures and concepts:** variable condition. Here the hard part is often knowing _when_ to apply them (p. 11).
- **Principles:** verbal KCs with a rationale, such as theorems. Deductive proof sits under sense-making (p. 22).

**Hypothesised links to learning.** Memory processes such as spacing and retrieval matter for every kind. Induction and refinement matter for rules and principles. Sense-making matters only for principles (Table 4, p. 23). Two of these points have practical consequences for us:

- Mastery of a rule can only be inferred from _varied_ tasks, because students induce over-general conditions such as "angles that look equal are equal" (pp. 13, 17).
- Prerequisite structure matters mainly for sense-making knowledge, while plain sets of KCs suffice for memory and fluency ([Pelánek 2017](../sources/pelanek-2017-learner-modeling-overview.md), p. 332).

**How strong this is.** The KLI authors call these links "tentative hypotheses" that "require further testing" (p. 18), and the table of experimental support behind them is sparse (Table 6, p. 28). Pelánek's claim that forgetting matters more for facts is a hypothesis too (p. 343).

The one robust behavioural consequence, spaced retrieval for everything, needs no taxonomy. No source tests whether an LLM can label kinds reliably. The kind is therefore at most a hint for item design: varied surface forms for rules, explain-why items for principles. It should not switch the scheduler or the mastery logic.

**Concepts and procedures.** In math, procedural knowledge builds conceptual knowledge as well as the reverse. No study supports teaching concepts fully before procedures, and alternating them did at least as well in the one order-only study ([Rittle-Johnson 2015](../sources/rittle-johnson-2015-procedural-conceptual.md), pp. 590–594). A concept → procedure link should therefore never lock the procedure.

This evidence is on children, and university math with proofs is not covered by any source here.

Grade: **D**. A strong framework, but the knowledge-by-practice links are untested hypotheses, the evidence comes from school settings, and kind labelling by an LLM is untested.

### How accurately can LLMs extract concepts and prerequisite links, compared to an expert map?

**KC labels.**

- **Match with the course authors ([Moore 2024](../sources/moore-2024-llm-kc-generation.md), p. 128).** GPT-4's single KC, from multiple-choice questions alone, matched the authors' KC for 56% of undergraduate Chemistry and 35% of master's E-Learning questions. The authors' KC was in its top five for 79% and 63%.
- **Mismatch is not error.** On mismatches, raters preferred the LLM label 57 of 87 times (p = .017, p. 129). The preference is itself weak: half the raters were crowd workers, unanimity was low, and readability may have driven it (p. 130).
- **Fit to student data.** In an introductory programming course, KCs from GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 produced learning curves "as smooth or smoother than" expert KCs, and were named consistently across assignments ([Fan 2025](../sources/fan-2025-llm-kc-learning-curves.md), abstract only). This test against student data is the one that matters.
- **The opposite result.** Raw KCs from GPT-4o-mini fit student data worse than expert KCs and were five times as many. Merging near-duplicates closed part of the gap ([Wang 2025](../sources/wang-2025-llm-kc-identification.md), abstract only, preprint).

**The grain error runs both ways.** Moore found the LLM "often favors general options over precise and domain-specific ones" (p. 130). Wang found far too many KCs. Moore also found that the same KC gets different wordings on different questions (p. 127), and that niche content does worse than introductory Chemistry (pp. 130–131).

So the direction depends on model, prompt and domain, and the pipeline needs both a merge step and a check for KCs that are too broad.

**Prerequisite links.** Given a fixed, expert-curated concept list, GPT-4 judged prerequisite pairs with accuracy 0.75 and F1 0.78. That beats classical supervised methods but trails a supervised graph model at 0.81 ([Yang 2024](../sources/yang-2024-llm-concept-graph-recovery.md), p. 4).

That number is optimistic for us, for three reasons:

- The benchmark may be in GPT's training data (p. 4).
- The concepts were given, not extracted.
- A real course map is sparse, so most candidate pairs are not prerequisites. The same per-pair error rate on a sparse graph gives a precision well below 75%. This is our reasoning; the paper reports neither the pair ratio nor precision.

Yang also found that adding lecture notes to the prompt lowered performance (LLaMa, NLP, attributed to length; p. 7). That is one model on one domain, too thin to design around.

**Expert maps are not a gold standard.** Experts miss distinctions that matter to novices because much of their knowledge is non-verbal ([Koedinger 2012](../sources/koedinger-2012-kli-framework.md), pp. 7, 14; [Liu 2017](../sources/liu-2017-closing-the-loop.md), p. 26). The goal is a map on which practice data behaves well, not agreement with an expert.

**What no study did:**

- No study built a map with prerequisites end to end from one course's lecture material and checked it against students or an exam.
- None used math-heavy material, where KCs are mostly procedures and derivations rather than named concepts.

Grade: **C**. University content and current models, but few small studies, two of them abstract-only, none on math, and none on the full task.

### Can practice data validate and repair the map after launch, and how much data does that need?

**With many students on one map, yes.**

- **A tested method.** Learning Factors Analysis searches splits and merges of an existing KC model against student data. It improved KC models across 11 datasets, as Liu & Koedinger report from earlier work ([Liu 2017](../sources/liu-2017-closing-the-loop.md), p. 27). In the one closed-loop test, the improvement it found raised learning in a randomised classroom comparison (pp. 33–34).
- **Agreement with teachers.** A simple data-driven measure of prerequisite strength largely agreed with teachers ([Adjei 2016](../sources/adjei-2016-prerequisite-skill-data.md), abstract only).
- **LLM KCs judged by student data.** Learning curves can judge LLM KCs as well as expert ones ([Fan 2025](../sources/fan-2025-llm-kc-learning-curves.md)).

**The limits are severe for our launch setting:**

- **Data volume.** Validation needed tens of thousands of steps (Liu 2017, p. 28). Fit-based checks "often require large amounts of data" ([Moore 2024](../sources/moore-2024-llm-kc-generation.md), p. 123).
- **Tiny margins.** The better model differed in the third decimal of RMSE (Liu 2017, p. 30).
- **Only local fixes.** The method needs a starting model and "cannot produce large structural changes" (p. 37).
- **One student per course.** With one student per uploaded course, none of this is available at launch. Map quality rests on the pipeline.

Within one student's data, the most the app can do is flag suspects for review. Two learning-curve symptoms described by Moore (p. 123) and Liu (p. 27) qualify:

- a KC whose correctness does not improve over opportunities
- a KC that is failed on one task type and passed on others

**Cheap quality checks before any student data exists** have no direct evidence in this question. Examples are coverage of the course's exercises and old exam tasks, and whether every KC has a source chunk and a task. They follow from the KC definition, which requires each KC to be visible in tasks, and are graded D below.

Grade: **D** for our setting: one closed-loop experiment in high-school geometry, data volumes far beyond one student, and no evidence on pre-data checks.

## Challenges

One skeptic pass, seven points. All were accepted in substance.

1. **Fine KCs are unreliable for one student with days of data.** Accepted. The draft said "track mastery on KCs". The answer now separates the tagging unit (KC) from the estimation and reporting unit (topic), with KC estimates borrowing from their topic and readiness shown with uncertainty. Liu 2017 is now read as evidence that unaddressed sub-skills cost learning, not that per-KC estimates help.
2. **The knowledge-kind taxonomy cannot drive product behaviour at grade C.** Accepted. Sub-question 2 is regraded D. The kind is now a hint for item design, not a branch in scheduling or mastery logic, and the Answer no longer states it as settled. LLM kind labelling is untested and becomes a measurement in [DR-003](../design-rules/DR-003-knowledge-kind-as-item-hint.md).
3. **"One wrong prerequisite judgment in four" is over- and under-read.** Accepted. The Answer no longer cites 75% as a working number. The sub-question now names why it is optimistic: possible contamination, given concepts, and sparse graphs. The lecture-notes finding is marked as too thin to design around.
4. **Newer work and the direction of LLM grain errors.** Accepted. [Fan 2025](../sources/fan-2025-llm-kc-learning-curves.md) was added (the 10th card, abstract only). The synthesis now says the grain error runs both ways depending on model and domain, and treats Moore's mismatches as disagreements, not errors. Moore and Wang were downgraded to medium transfer: they tag multiple-choice items with named concepts, not math procedures.
5. **The opportunities-per-KC arithmetic built on an abstract.** Accepted. The "8 to 16 opportunities" and "800 to 1,600" figures were removed. Only the qualitative bound remains, and no rule sets a numeric cap on map size.
6. **Repair rests on one experiment and skips pre-data quality checks.** Accepted. Sub-question 4 is regraded D for our setting. Pre-data checks (coverage of exercises and old exams, every KC anchored on a source chunk and a task) are stated as reasoning without direct evidence, graded D in [DR-002](../design-rules/DR-002-build-map-from-anchored-kcs.md), and listed under Gaps. The 11-dataset LFA result is now attributed as Liu & Koedinger's report of earlier work.
7. **Grades ignore transfer distance; university math is absent.** Accepted. Grades now differ by sub-question: C for unit and extraction, D for kinds and repair. The absence of university math is listed as a central gap, not a footnote.

## Gaps

- **No end-to-end study.** Nothing tests an LLM building a KC map with prerequisites from one course's own lecture material and validates it against students or a delayed exam. This is the central uncertainty, and it can only be measured in the app.
- **University math and proofs.** No source read uses university math or proof-based content. The knowledge-kind evidence comes from school math, science and language; the LLM evidence from Chemistry, E-Learning, programming and NLP.
- **Pre-data quality checks.** There is no evidence on cheap ways to validate a map before student data exists, such as coverage of old exam tasks or LLM self-consistency across runs.
- **Granularity numbers.** How many opportunities a KC needs before an exam, and with what spread, would come from the full text of [Koedinger 2023](../sources/koedinger-2023-learning-rate-regularity.md). That is the first thing to deepen.
- **Leads not followed:**
  - Nathan & Koedinger (2000), doi:10.2307/749750, on expert blind spot, cited here only second-hand
  - An LLM labelling of atomic learning objectives in physics, doi:10.1145/3706468.3706550, suggested by the skeptic, not read
- **Caps:**

## Rules

- [DR-001](../design-rules/DR-001-kcs-for-tagging-topics-for-estimates.md): Tag everything to knowledge components; estimate and report readiness on topics. Grade C.
- [DR-002](../design-rules/DR-002-build-map-from-anchored-kcs.md): Build the map from task-anchored, de-duplicated KCs with soft prerequisite edges. Grade C.
- [DR-003](../design-rules/DR-003-knowledge-kind-as-item-hint.md): Use a KC's knowledge kind only as a hint for item design. Grade D.
- [DR-004](../design-rules/DR-004-versioned-map-and-kc-event-log.md): Version the map and log attempts against stable KC ids so it can be repaired from pooled data. Grade D.
