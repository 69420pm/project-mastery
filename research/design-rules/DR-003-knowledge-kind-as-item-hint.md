# Use a KC's knowledge kind only as a hint for item design

Each KC may carry a kind: fact or definition, rule or procedure, or principle (a theorem or a reason with a rationale). The item bank uses it as a hint:

- **Rules:** items in varied surface forms, so mastery is not inferred from one problem type.
- **Principles:** explain-why and justify items next to applications.
- **Facts:** short recall items.

The kind does not change the scheduler or the mastery logic. Every KC gets spaced retrieval practice.

**Rationale:** The KLI framework links kinds of knowledge to kinds of learning: memory processes help every kind, induction helps rules, sense-making helps principles. It also argues that a rule's mastery can only be inferred from varied tasks. But its authors call these links tentative hypotheses with sparse experimental support, the evidence is from school settings, and no study tests whether an LLM labels kinds reliably ([Q-013](../syntheses/Q-013.md), sub-question 2). Only the cheap, low-risk use is justified.

**Boundaries:** Proof-based content is not covered by the evidence. Treat proofs as principles until a dedicated question says otherwise.

**What would change our mind:** Evidence that scheduling or mastery thresholds tuned per kind improve delayed exam results in university STEM; or app data showing that LLM kind labels are unstable across runs, in which case drop the field.

**How to measure in the app:**

- Label kinds twice with different runs or models on the eval courses and measure agreement.
- Compare, per kind, whether students who are marked mastered on varied items do better on unseen mock-exam items than those mastered on a single item type.
