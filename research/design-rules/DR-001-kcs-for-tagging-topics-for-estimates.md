# Tag everything to knowledge components; estimate and report readiness on topics

The topic map has two layers. The lower layer is knowledge components (KCs): sub-skills at the level where students in the course still make errors, such as "find the radius from a circle's area". Items, hints, source chunks and every attempt are tagged to KCs. The upper layer is topics that group KCs. The planner prioritises topics, and the readiness view reports per topic as a range. KC-level estimates borrow strength from their topic. At KC level the student sees coverage ("practised / not yet practised"), not a mastery number.

**Rationale:** A KC is defined by the tasks that show it, at the grain just above what novices can already do ([Q-013](../syntheses/Q-013.md), sub-question 1). Naming a hidden sub-skill matters: targeting one improved post-test scores from 0.73 to 0.85 in a classroom RCT. But finer KCs mean fewer data per KC and unstable estimates. With one student and days to weeks of practice, per-KC estimates would mostly show their prior, so estimates and readiness belong on the coarser layer.

**Boundaries:** For facts and definitions practised as flashcards, the KC and its review item can coincide, and per-KC memory state is fine because each KC gets many cheap repetitions. Once many students share a course map, KC-level estimates become reliable sooner and can be shown.

**What would change our mind:** Evidence or app data that per-KC estimates from one student's practice predict exam items as well as topic estimates do; or that topic-level readiness hides weaknesses that later show up on the exam.

**How to measure in the app:** Compare topic-level and KC-level estimates on how well they predict held-out practice items and mock-exam items per student (calibration and log loss). Track how many attempts a KC has when it is first reported as mastered.
