# Koedinger, Carvalho, Liu & McLaughlin (2023). An astonishing regularity in student learning rate

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 120(13). https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2221311120

- **Design and population:** secondary analysis of 1.3 million observations across 27 datasets from online practice systems, elementary school to college, in math, science and language
- **Intervention vs. comparison:** none; statistical models estimate initial correctness and learning rate per student and per skill component, on groups of tasks that assess the same skill component and give follow-up instruction on errors
- **Outcome and delay:** correctness per practice opportunity within the practice system; no delayed test
- **Effect:** initial accuracy about 65% despite prior lectures and readings (about 55% in the lower half of students, 75% in the upper half); learning rate "astonishingly similar", about 0.1 log odds or 2.5% accuracy per opportunity
- **Boundary conditions:** depends on a well-specified skill-component model per dataset (the DataShop KC models); how many opportunities to reach mastery to be read in the full text if this card is deepened
- **Quality notes:** large, multi-dataset; observational modelling, so learning rate is a model parameter, not a measured outcome
- **Transfer notes:** includes college courses and math, but practice in step-based tutors with immediate feedback, not open-ended exam problems; no delayed exam

> "students demonstrate modest initial prepractice performance, at about 65% accuracy" (abstract)
