# Tutoring scenarios on Muse Ember, 2026-11-12/13

Issue #277. The ten scenarios in `evals/tutor/scenarios/` ran on `meta/muse-ember` through `pnpm tutor-eval`, on the gold course `linear-algebra-I`. For each run, a Claude Opus 5.5 subagent played the Student from the scenario's persona and moves, then scored every checklist item 0 to 2 (2 = met, 1 = partly or late, 0 = not met) and wrote a note on each reply. Subagents ran one at a time because of the 5 requests per minute limit. A scenario whose checklist hangs on a single tool call ran twice per round, and those rows show the mean.

## Changes and their reasons

Each change names the general problem it fixes. None of them names Course content.

**Code fixes (deterministic bugs the runs exposed):**

- `65b4c88` fix(chat): Muse Ember takes only `toolChoice: "auto"`. The Chat's and the Brief's last step sent `"none"`, which got a 400, "only `"auto"` is supported for `tool_choice`". That cut off every reply that reached its fifth step (round 0 of 02 was rerun after the fix). With `activeTools: []` the model still calls a tool, so its last step can't be forced to text. `textOnlyStep` in `src/lib/ai/models.ts` leaves it on auto for such models.
- `1be2024` (runner): each `say` is its own process, so its pacing could not see the requests of the `say` before it. The request times now live in a file in the temp directory.
- `d885910` (runner): step texts were joined without a break, so two steps read as one run-on sentence, which the Chat never shows.

**Round 1 (`41bf6d0`), from round 0's problems:**

- Replies used up their five steps (failed `compute` calls, extra lookups) and ended on "here's one more:" with no `ask`. Added: a reply has room for four tool calls, and a reply that announces a question ends with `ask`.
- Text written before tool calls was restated after them. Added: write at most one short sentence before the calls (replaced in round 2).
- Errors were corrected for the Student: the right value, the whole matrix, the counterexample. Added: point to where the error is and let the Student fix it.
- Questions asked what the reply had just explained, and several new ideas came in one reply. Added: ask before you explain, one new idea per reply.
- Due Reviews were dropped during exam work and left out of plans. Added: due Reviews in every session and first in any plan.
- Grading gave one result to every KC of an answer, gave full without checking each condition, and graded skipped or recited answers. Added: grade each KC on its own part against its source's conditions, and grade only what the Student wrote.
- A side question ("where is that in the script?") closed the open Question. Added: a side question leaves it open.
- Citation links were placeholders ("(link)", "link from source"), `example.com`, or chunk ids that do not exist. Added: copy the link a tool result gave character for character, or give no link.
- Internal terms reached the Student ("KC", "band", "strong"). Added: never show the app's terms for their data.
- Exam scope was answered from the script alone. Added: check the course's exam information and old exams first.
- `compute` named matrices `A`, `B`, `C` and a parameter `t`, which are mathjs units, and called `rank` and `inverse`. The tool description now lists the single letters that are units and the matrix functions (`inv`, no `rank`), and the refusal says "A1 for A, and run the program again".

**Round 2 (`dd35316`, then `0963929`), from round 1's problems:**

- Narration ("I'll grade that first", "the system can't mark this full", "that ID was mistyped") reached the Student. It replaced the one-sentence rule: everything written reaches the Student as one message, so no narration and no promises of what comes next, other calls first, then the text, all of it before `ask` or `showReading`.
- Raw review dates were printed ("we'll come back to it on 2026-11-15"), copied from round 1's own example wording. The example now says "in a few days".
- Due Reviews were still skipped next to a new Topic. The rule now names exam work and a new Topic.
- Corrections did not say what was right, and questions held their own answer ("If not, give two vectors…"). Added: say what is right first, never hint at a question's answer, and use other values in an example than in the question.
- A Student insisting on a wrong claim got the general theorem, and "why does it hold?" got the proof. Added: give one concrete case to check, let them guess or prove a result of the course, and answer other direct questions first (the last clause came from the stopped first attempt, where check questions replaced answers).
- Long multi-part problems were used as "quick checks" of claimed knowledge, and promised plans never came. Added: one short question per idea, and the plan in the same reply.
- A restated definition missing a condition got full. `help` was `some` without hints, and not `solution_shown` when the solution came in the same reply. Grading and the `grade` help description now cover both.
- Part of a Problem was asked with its `problemId`, so `grade` needed values for parts never asked. After the "full needs a value" refusal, the model lowered correct results. The `problemId` description now says to omit it when asking only some parts. The refusal now says to give the value as the Student wrote it and keep the results.
- A question beyond the course went through `ask` and was graded. Added: such a question stays in text.
- The German Student never got the script's English term. Added: give the Materials' term beside a translated one the first time.

**Round 3 (`ee504eb`, reverted by `1e463de`):** the instructions said to start a reply with its tool calls and no text, to copy ids character for character and fix a refused call in the same reply, to ask a due Review next to a mock's tasks, and to show a contrasting case for two mixed-up ideas. The first rule backfired. Replies posted an exercise with no text and ignored the Student's direct question (05, twice in a row), or announced a check and ended without `ask` for two replies (02, both runs). The narration it targeted stayed.

## What stays weak on Muse Ember

- **Narration.** In every round, most replies open with a sentence before their tool calls ("I'll grade that answer first") and then restate it. No wording removed it, and the strongest wording (round 3) broke replies.
- **A question announced but not asked.** This happens in about one run in five: the reply ends on "quick check:" without `ask`, or an `ask` refused for a typed id is not retried.
- **`help` labels.** `some` for answers given without hints, and `none` when the same reply shows the solution. This held in most runs of 02 and 03 in every round.
- **Due Reviews next to focused work** (07 in every round), and **citing the Exercise's own page** when grading it (02 in every round).
- **Grading a mismatch.** A wrong final value turns every `full` of the answer into `partial`, including the KC of a correct step. That is the `grade` tool's rule (`tools.ts`), not the model's doing. The judges marked it down in 05 and 10, and it stays as designed.
